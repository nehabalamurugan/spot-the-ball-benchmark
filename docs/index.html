Spot The Ball: A Benchmark for Visual Social Inference in Sports Scenes

1Stanford Computer Science    2Stanford Psychology

Abstract

Vision–language models (VLMs) are increasingly used in socially grounded perception tasks, yet it remains unclear whether they can perform human-like social inference from visual cues alone. We introduce Spot The Ball, a benchmark designed to evaluate visual social reasoning by asking models to infer the location of an intentionally removed ball in soccer, basketball, and volleyball scenes using cues such as gaze, body orientation, and spatial role alignment. We compare four leading VLMs (Gemini, GPT, LLaMA, Qwen) against human performance across multiple prompting strategies and analyze systematic error patterns. Despite heavy exposure to sports scenes in web-scale pretraining, current models fall significantly short of human inference ability, revealing a persistent gap in embodied social understanding.
